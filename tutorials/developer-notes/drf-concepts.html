Developer Notes: DRF Concepts

Serializers, ViewSets, routers, permissions — taught with TaskViewSet and friends.

Serializers validate, they don't decide business rules

TaskSerializer.validate_labels checks a structural invariant (a label must belong to the same project as the task) — a shape/consistency rule, not a business decision. The actual decision of "is this task allowed to move to this state" lives in WorkflowService.transition_task, not in a serializer. Keeping that line clear is why the service layer exists.

ViewSets vs. plain APIViews

Anything with real CRUD (TaskViewSet, ProjectViewSet, …) is a ModelViewSet, registered on the router. Anything that isn't resource CRUD — LoginView, SearchView, LogoutView — is a plain APIView or a SimpleJWT view, wired into urls.py directly rather than forced onto a router it doesn't fit.

get_queryset() is the security boundary, not get_object()

Every viewset overrides get_queryset() to filter by the caller's membership before DRF ever looks up a specific object. That's what makes a non-member's request 404 rather than 403 — the object was never reachable to begin with. See Codebase → Permissions.

Composing mixins instead of inheriting a big class

NotificationViewSet(mixins.ListModelMixin, mixins.RetrieveModelMixin, GenericViewSet) picks exactly the two operations notifications support (list, retrieve) instead of inheriting a full ModelViewSet and disabling the rest.
